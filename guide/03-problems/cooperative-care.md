# Cooperative care: vet visits, nail trims, grooming

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus for the training; Thin to Moderate for pre-visit medication (small trials).
**See a vet first?** The vet is the point. Tell the clinic you are working on this; many will schedule "happy visits".

## What it is

Teaching the dog to accept, and ideally to signal readiness for, handling it does not enjoy: nail trims, ear cleaning, injections, exams, brushing. The goal is a dog that holds still because it has learned the procedure is safe and that it can pause it, not a dog that is pinned.

## Before you start

- List what the dog struggles with and how it shows it (pulling the paw, lip licking, growling, snapping).
- Work with a professional if there has been a bite over handling.
- For dogs that need a procedure before training is done, ask the clinic about sedation or pre-visit medication rather than forcing it. One forced trim can undo months.

## Do this

1. **Teach a start button.** A chin rest on your palm or a towel: the dog places its chin, you touch a paw. The dog lifting its chin means "pause", and you pause. The dog learns it has a say, and dogs with a say stay calmer.
2. **Break the procedure into tiny steps.** For nails: see the clippers, clippers touch the paw, clippers around one nail, one nail clipped. Each step gets a treat, many times, before the next.
3. **Pair handling with food.** Touch the ear, treat. Lift the lip, treat. Hold the paw for one second, treat. Build duration slowly. Card: [`guide/04-protocols/counterconditioning-and-desensitization.md`](../04-protocols/counterconditioning-and-desensitization.md).
4. **Happy visits.** Walk into the clinic, get treats from staff, weigh in, leave. Nothing else. The building stops predicting needles.
5. **Ask for low-stress handling.** Many clinics follow Fear Free principles: towels, treats, no restraint by force, letting the dog choose the floor over the table.

Starting numbers: 3 to 5 minute sessions, several times a week. One nail a day is fine. Adjust to your dog.

## Medication before visits, in plain terms

| Drug | What the small trials found |
|---|---|
| Trazodone (single dose) | Lowered some stress scores in a 20-dog crossover trial; stress hormone unchanged. Thin to Moderate. |
| Gabapentin (single dose) | Only lip licking fell in a 22-dog trial. Thin. |
| Dexmedetomidine gel | Reduced fear during minor procedures in a 74 to 76 dog trial, and reduced stress signs (vocalizing, avoidance, panting, trembling) during exams in a 40-dog crossover trial. Both involved the manufacturer. The higher dose raised heart concerns in a few dogs. Moderate. |
| Combinations | A 45-dog study of a multi-drug protocol lowered stress scores but had no placebo group. Thin. |

No doses here. Your vet decides, and multi-drug approaches currently have better support than single agents.

## Avoid this

- **Holding the dog down to "just get it done"**: it works once and teaches the dog to fight harder.
- **Punishing the growl during handling**: the next warning is a bite.
- **Skipping nails until they are a crisis**: short, frequent sessions beat one long battle.

## How long it takes

Weeks to months, depending on history. A dog that has been forced before takes longer than a puppy starting fresh.

## Get help if

- Any bite during handling
- The dog needs a procedure now and cannot tolerate it (ask the vet about sedation, not force)
