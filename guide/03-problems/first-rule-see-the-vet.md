# First rule: see the vet

Read this before any other problem page. Two minutes.

**Evidence for this page:** Moderate. A review of behavior referral caseloads found pain involved in roughly a third of cases, and in some clinics far more.

## Why

A dog in pain gets grumpy, guards things, snaps when touched, refuses to sit, pulls away from the leash, barks at nothing, or stops being able to hold its bladder. Every one of those looks like a training problem. Training a dog in pain does not work, and punishing a dog in pain is cruel.

Pain hides. Dogs rarely limp or cry from the kinds of pain that drive behavior: sore hips, a bad back, an aching mouth, an upset gut, itchy skin.

## What to tell the vet

- What the dog does, when it started, and what changed around that time
- Whether the behavior is worse after exercise, rest, cold weather, or being touched in a certain place
- Any change in sleep, appetite, stool, scratching, or willingness to jump, climb stairs, or play
- Bring video. Dogs behave differently in the clinic.

## What a thorough check covers

| Area | Why it matters for behavior |
|---|---|
| Joints, spine, muscles | The most common hidden driver. Watch for reluctance to sit squarely, stand, or be touched over the back or hips |
| Mouth and teeth | Mouthing, chewing, face-touch aggression, dropped food |
| Gut | Grass eating, lip licking, restlessness at night, irritability |
| Skin and ears | Scratching, head shaking, touch sensitivity |
| Thyroid and other blood work | Energy, weight, and mood changes |
| Eyes and ears | A dog that cannot see or hear well startles and reacts |
| Bladder and kidneys | Any house-training regression in an adult dog |

## A pain trial

If nothing shows but the vet still suspects pain, a short course of pain relief (often several weeks) while you watch the behavior is a legitimate diagnostic tool. If the behavior improves, you have your answer. Ask about it.

## When the vet says "it's behavioral"

That is a start, not an end. Ask for a referral to a veterinary behaviorist if the problem involves aggression, panic when alone, or fear that does not respond to the basics in this guide within 8 to 12 weeks. See [`guide/01-start-here/when-to-get-help.md`](../01-start-here/when-to-get-help.md).
