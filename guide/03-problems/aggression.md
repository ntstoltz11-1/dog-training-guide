# Aggression

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus for the plan; Moderate against confrontational methods; the bite scale is a widely used professional tool.
**See a vet first?** Yes, always. Pain is a leading hidden cause of aggression, and most aggression cases need a veterinary behaviorist.

## Safety comes before everything on this page

- A dog that has bitten a person needs a professional. Not a trainer found online: a veterinary behaviorist, or a certified behavior consultant working with your vet. [`guide/01-start-here/when-to-get-help.md`](../01-start-here/when-to-get-help.md).
- Children in the home change the math. A plan that is acceptable for an adult-only household may not be safe with children.
- Until you have a plan, prevent every situation that has led to aggression. Gates, leashes, separate rooms, no visitors.

## What it is

Growling, snapping, or biting directed at people or animals. Nearly all of it is driven by fear, pain, frustration, or defense of something the dog values. "Dominance aggression" is not a useful category; see [`guide/02-methods/dominance-and-compulsion.md`](../02-methods/dominance-and-compulsion.md).

Common types: fear-based (stranger approaches, hand reaches), pain-related (touched where it hurts), guarding (food, toys, people, space), frustration (barrier, leash), redirected (grabs whoever is nearest when aroused), dog-dog.

## How bad is it? The bite scale

| Level | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1 | Snap, no contact |
| 2 | Teeth touched skin, no puncture |
| 3 | One to four shallow punctures from a single bite |
| 4 | Deep punctures, or the dog held on and shook |
| 5 | Several level 4 bites in one incident |
| 6 | Death |

Levels 1 and 2 mean the dog has good bite control. From level 3 up, the outlook is more serious and professional help is not optional.

## Before you start (with your professional)

- A full medical work-up, including pain.
- A written list of every trigger: what, where, who, how close, and what the dog did.
- Muzzle training with rewards, so the dog is comfortable in a basket muzzle before it is needed. Card: [`guide/04-protocols/muzzle-training.md`](../04-protocols/muzzle-training.md).
- Clear management rules the whole household follows.

## What the plan usually contains

1. Management to stop rehearsals.
2. Counterconditioning: the trigger appears at a safe distance, great things follow, the feeling changes over time. Card: [`guide/04-protocols/counterconditioning-and-desensitization.md`](../04-protocols/counterconditioning-and-desensitization.md).
3. Teaching an alternative behavior for the trigger moment (go to mat, look at me, move away).
4. Cooperative care if handling is a trigger ([`cooperative-care.md`](cooperative-care.md)).
5. Medication where fear or arousal is too high for learning. A vet decides.

## Avoid this

- **Punishing the growl.** The growl is the warning. Remove it and you get a dog that bites "out of nowhere".
- **Alpha rolls, stare-downs, scruffing, hitting.** In one clinic survey, owners reported an aggressive response to 26 to 43% of uses of these techniques.
- **Flooding**: forcing the dog into the scary situation to "get over it".
- **Board-and-train that excludes you.** The aggression lives in your home, with you.

## How long it takes

Months to lifelong management. Many dogs improve a great deal; some are managed safely rather than cured. A good professional will tell you which is realistic and what "safe enough" looks like for your household.

## Get help if

You are reading this page. Aggression is the one problem in this guide that should not be self-managed.
