# Jumping up

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus. The learning principles are well established; no formal trial on jumping specifically.
**See a vet first?** Rarely needed, unless jumping is new and frantic in an adult dog.

## What it is

The dog jumps on people to greet them. It works: people look, talk, push, and touch, all of which are attention. Jumping is a greeting that has been rewarded hundreds of times.

## Before you start

- Agree as a household: nobody rewards jumping, not even with "off!" Shouting is attention.
- Put the dog on a leash before guests arrive, or behind a gate, so jumping cannot happen while you train.

## Do this

1. Decide what you want instead: four paws on the floor, or a sit. Four on the floor is easier to start with.
2. When the dog approaches with paws down, mark ("yes") and feed **low**, at the dog's chest height or on the floor, so the reward keeps the dog down.
3. If the dog jumps, turn your side or back, say nothing, and wait. The second the paws hit the floor, mark and feed low. The dog learns which position produces the person.
4. Practice with family first, then one calm visitor at a time, with the dog on leash so it cannot rehearse. Ask the visitor to toss treats on the floor as they approach.
5. Raise the difficulty: more excited greetings, people coming through the door, people with bags.

Starting numbers: 10 repetitions per guest, several guests a week. Adjust to your dog.

## Avoid this

- **Kneeing, pushing, or stepping on toes**: it is attention, and it can hurt the dog and sour greetings.
- **Letting some people allow it** ("I don't mind!"): the dog cannot tell which people mind. Everyone or no one.
- **Asking for a sit before the dog can think**: an excited dog may not be able to sit; four paws down is the first step.

## How long it takes

Two to six weeks if everyone is consistent. The spike before it gets better is normal: a dog that always got attention for jumping will jump harder for a few days when it stops working.

## Get help if

- Jumping comes with mouthing that leaves marks
- The dog knocks over children or elderly people and management is failing
