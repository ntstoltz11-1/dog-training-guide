# Recall (coming when called)

**Evidence for this page:** Moderate. One of the few behaviors tested in a controlled field study, which found reward-based recall training as good as or better than shock-collar training.
**See a vet first?** If a previously reliable recall fails suddenly. Hearing loss and pain both cause it.

## What it is

The dog comes when called, every time, even with distractions. Most "bad recall" is a cue that has been ruined: called to end the fun, called for a bath, called and ignored a hundred times until the word means nothing.

## Before you start

- **Pick a new word** if the old one is poisoned. "Come" has history; "here" or a whistle starts clean.
- **Long line** (5 to 10 metres on a harness) for all practice outside until the recall is proven. No more chances to ignore you.
- Find the best reward your dog knows: roast chicken, cheese, a tug game, a chase. Recall pays better than anything else.

## Do this

1. **Charge the word.** At home, say it once in a happy voice and feed a jackpot (several treats, one at a time). Ten times a day for a few days. The word now predicts the best thing.
2. **Short distances, no distractions.** Say the word when the dog is already heading your way. Party when it arrives. Never say it when you think the dog will not come.
3. **Release back to fun.** After most recalls, say "go play" and let the dog go. Coming to you does not end the walk; it is a brief pit stop. This is the single most powerful trick in recall training.
4. **Add distance, then distraction**, one at a time, on the long line. If the dog fails, the line stops it from self-rewarding; walk up, show the treat, bring it back, and make the next one easier.
5. **Games.** Hide and seek indoors; two people calling the dog back and forth; "catch me" where you run away as you call.
6. **Pay every time, for life.** Real-life rewards count (the door opens, the ball is thrown), but the recall word never goes unpaid.

Starting numbers: 10 easy recalls a day; a 10 to 1 ratio of recalls that release back to fun versus recalls that end it. Adjust to your dog.

## Avoid this

- **Calling for anything unpleasant**: nails, bath, crate, leaving the park. Go and get the dog instead.
- **Repeating the cue**: "Come. Come! COME!" teaches that the first two do not count.
- **Scolding the dog when it finally arrives**: the dog was punished for coming.
- **Shock collars**: in the one direct comparison (63 dogs), reward-trained dogs obeyed the first "come" more often and responded faster. That study has a published critique and an author reply; the result is argued over, but nobody has shown shock collars doing better.

## How long it takes

One to six months to a solid recall around normal distractions. Wildlife and other dogs are the final exam and may always need a long line for some dogs. That is management, not failure.

## Get help if

- The dog runs toward roads
- Recall fails only around specific triggers (see [Leash reactivity (barking and lunging at dogs or people)](leash-reactivity.md))
