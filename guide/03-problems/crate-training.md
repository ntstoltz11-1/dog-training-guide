# Crate training

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus. Confinement training is endorsed in veterinary position statements; the caution about crates and separation panic is Thin but important.
**See a vet first?** Not usually. If the dog panics in the crate, read `separation-anxiety.md` before continuing.

## What it is

Teaching the dog that a crate is a safe, pleasant den it chooses to rest in. Done well, it helps house training, travel, vet stays, and recovery after surgery. Done badly, it becomes a cage the dog fears.

## Before you start

- Size: the dog can stand, turn around, and lie flat. Larger is not better for house training.
- Place it where the family is, not in a garage or basement.
- Remove collars with tags in the crate (strangling risk) or use a breakaway collar.

## Do this

1. **Door open, no pressure.** Toss treats in. Let the dog go in and out freely for a day or two. Feed meals in the crate with the door open.
2. **Door closed, you present.** Close the door while the dog eats a chew. Open it before the dog finishes. Repeat over several sessions, closing it for a little longer each time.
3. **Door closed, you move.** Step away, come back, open. Then leave the room for seconds, then a minute. Return before the dog fusses.
4. **Build to real absences** only once the dog settles with you out of sight for 10 to 15 minutes.
5. Make the crate the best place in the house: special chews that only appear there, a soft bed, a cover if the dog likes a den.

Starting numbers: five minutes a session, several sessions a day, over one to four weeks. Adjust to your dog.

## Avoid this

- **Using the crate as punishment**: it must only predict good things.
- **Crating a panicking dog "until it gets used to it"**: dogs with separation anxiety can break teeth and nails trying to escape. There is no good evidence that crates help separation panic. Video the dog; if it is calmer loose in a safe room, use the room.
- **Too long**: adult dogs should not spend the whole workday crated. Puppies far less.

## How long it takes

One to four weeks for a dog to settle happily. Puppies often adapt in days.

## Get help if

- The dog injures itself in the crate
- The dog drools, pants, or soils only when crated
