# Separation anxiety

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus for the training plan; Moderate to Strong for medication (the best drug trials in dog behavior are for this problem).
**See a vet first?** Yes. Medication is often part of the plan, and some "separation" problems are pain, bladder issues, or noise fear that happens while you are out.

## What it is

Panic when left alone, or when a specific person leaves. Signs within minutes of departure: howling, barking, pacing, drooling, soiling, destruction aimed at doors and windows, refusing food left out, injuries from escape attempts. It is not spite, and it is not boredom. A bored dog chews the sofa cushion; a panicking dog chews the door frame.

## Before you start

- **Video the dog alone.** Most owners misread the cause. Ten minutes of footage tells you whether this is panic, boredom, noise, or a bladder problem.
- **Suspend absences longer than the dog can handle.** This is the hard part and the whole foundation. Use a sitter, daycare, a friend, or take the dog with you while you train. Every panic rehearsal sets you back.
- Find the **threshold**: how many seconds alone before the first stress sign on video. Often it is under a minute. That is your starting point.

## Do this

1. Make departures boring. No long goodbyes, no dramatic returns.
2. Practice graduated departures, starting **below** threshold. If the dog panics at 40 seconds, start at 10. Step out, come back, repeat. Card: `guide/04-protocols/graduated-departures.md`.
3. Watch on video and extend by seconds, then minutes, only while the dog stays calm. Mix easy short reps with the longer ones so the pattern is not "always longer".
4. Several short sessions a week, with rest days. Progress is measured in minutes alone before the first stress sign.
5. Talk to your vet about medication. It lowers the panic enough for the training to work; it does not replace it.

Starting numbers: 3 to 5 sessions a week, 20 to 30 minutes each including breaks. Adjust to your dog.

## Medication, in plain terms

- **Fluoxetine** plus a behavior plan: in a randomized trial of over 200 dogs, 72% improved versus 50% on placebo. Note that half the placebo dogs also improved; the training does much of the work.
- **Clomipramine** plus behavior therapy: one randomized trial (95 dogs, funded by the maker) found the standard dose sped up improvement in destruction and house-soiling, but not in vocalizing. An earlier one (49 dogs) found the drug did not change the separation signs themselves, while the behavior therapy alone worked very well. Mixed.
- Both take 4 to 6 weeks to reach full effect. No doses here; your vet decides.

## About crates

There is no good evidence that crates help separation anxiety, and some panicking dogs injure themselves trying to get out. Test with video. If your dog is calmer loose in a safe room than crated, that is your answer.

## Avoid this

- **"Cry it out"**: leaving a panicking dog to get used to it. It does not habituate; it sensitizes.
- **Punishing the mess or damage** when you get home. The dog connects your return with punishment and panics more.
- **Getting a second dog as a cure.** Sometimes it helps a dog that is lonely; it rarely helps true separation panic.

## How long it takes

Months. Many cases take 4 to 12 months of steady work. Early progress looks like going from 30 seconds to 5 minutes. Plateaus are normal; drop back to easier reps for a week.

## Get help if

- The dog injures itself
- You cannot arrange a way to avoid absences while training
- No increase in calm alone-time after 6 to 8 weeks
