# Impulse control and over-arousal

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus. Widely taught; research on these specific games is thin.
**See a vet first?** If the dog cannot settle at all, or arousal appeared suddenly in an adult dog. Pain and some medical conditions cause restlessness.

## What it is

A dog that cannot come down: jumping, mouthing, spinning, grabbing the leash, barking at you, zooming through the house, unable to lie still even when tired. Often an adolescent. The dog is not "bad"; it has not learned how to switch off, and may not be getting enough sleep.

## Before you start

- **Count sleep.** Adult dogs often need 12 or more hours a day; puppies and adolescents more. Many over-aroused dogs are chronically under-slept because the household is busy. A quiet crate or room with the lights low after exercise is training.
- Check the exercise balance. A dog run to exhaustion every day can get fitter and more wired, not calmer. Swap some running for sniffing walks and food puzzles.
- Remove the easy wins for arousal: no wrestling games on the floor, no high-pitched greetings.

## Do this

1. **Capture calm.** Whenever the dog lies down on its own, quietly drop a treat between its paws. No cue, no fuss. The dog learns that stillness pays.
2. **Mat work.** Teach "go to your mat" and reward relaxed positions (hip rolled, head down) over alert ones. Then run the Relaxation Protocol. Card: `guide/04-protocols/relaxation-protocol.md`.
3. **Self-control games.** Hold a treat in a closed fist; the dog gets it only when it stops pawing and backs off. Progress to an open hand, then a treat on the floor. The dog learns that leaving things alone makes them appear.
4. **Play with rules.** Tug is excellent for arousal training: "take it" starts the game, "out" pauses it (trade for a treat), a sit restarts it. The dog practices going up and coming down.
5. **Predictable routines.** Same sequence before walks, meals, and visitors. Predictability lowers arousal.

Starting numbers: 2 to 3 short sessions a day of 3 to 5 minutes. Adjust to your dog.

## Avoid this

- **More exercise as the only answer**: it can build an athlete who still cannot settle.
- **Shouting or pushing the dog off**: attention is attention; it feeds the loop.
- **Long training sessions**: an aroused dog learns in bursts, not marathons.

## How long it takes

Weeks to months, with adolescence as a complicating factor (see `guide/05-development/adolescence.md`). Progress looks like faster recovery after excitement and the dog choosing its mat on its own.

## Get help if

- Arousal tips into mouthing that breaks skin or biting
- The dog cannot settle even after enforced rest for several days
- Sudden onset in an adult dog
