# Other common problems: counter-surfing, digging, door-dashing, car trouble, poop eating

Five small problems that do not need a full page each. Same logic every time: what does the dog get out of it, how do you prevent it, what do you teach instead.

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus throughout. These are management-plus-reward solutions with no formal trials.
**See a vet first?** For poop eating in an adult dog (gut and nutrient issues) and for car sickness (nausea is treatable).

## Counter-surfing and stealing

**Why it works:** the kitchen counter is a slot machine. One sandwich every twenty tries keeps the dog checking forever.

1. **Make the counter pay nothing.** Clear it. Every time. This is the whole fix for most dogs; the training below only works if the counter stops paying.
2. Teach "go to your mat" in the kitchen and reward the dog heavily for lying there while you cook. Card: `guide/04-protocols/relaxation-protocol.md`.
3. Teach "leave it" and a cheerful "trade" for stolen items. Never chase; chasing is the best game in the house. `resource-guarding.md` has the trade steps.
4. Gate the kitchen when you cannot supervise.

**Avoid:** booby traps and shouting. They teach the dog to steal when you are not there.

## Digging

**Why it works:** cool earth on a hot day, buried smells, boredom, or den-building in a dog bred for it.

1. Give a legal digging spot: a sandbox or a corner of soft earth. Bury toys and treats there. Praise digging there.
2. Make other spots less inviting: chicken wire under the soil, rocks, or simply less unsupervised yard time.
3. Add enrichment: sniffing walks, food puzzles, chews. A dog with a job digs less.
4. If the dog digs along the fence line, it may be trying to get out: anxiety, a dog next door, or wildlife. Address that cause.

**Avoid:** filling holes with the dog's own poop, pepper, or other folk remedies. Unpleasant and pointless.

## Door-dashing

**Why it works:** the open door is the best thing that happens all day, and bolting through it has worked before.

1. Teach "wait" at a closed door: hand on the handle, dog sits, door opens a crack, closes if the dog moves, opens wider if the dog holds. Release with a word. Many short reps.
2. Put a gate or pen between the dog and the front door so dashing cannot happen while you train.
3. Make the door boring: open and close it many times a day with nothing happening.
4. Teach a strong recall as a backup. `recall.md`.

**Avoid:** chasing the escaped dog toward the road. Turn and run the other way, calling cheerfully; most dogs chase you.

## Car trouble: sickness, barking, panic

**Car sickness:** common in puppies and often outgrown. Ask the vet about anti-nausea medication; nausea becomes fear of the car fast if left. Then rebuild the car as a good place: sit in the parked car with treats, engine off; then engine on; then a trip round the block to somewhere fun.

**Barking at everything through the window:** crate or seatbelt harness so the dog cannot patrol, cover the crate or use window film, and reward quiet with treats at red lights. The visual barrier does most of the work.

**Panic in the car:** treat as fear. Start with the parked car and counterconditioning. Card: `guide/04-protocols/counterconditioning-and-desensitization.md`. If the dog shakes, drools, and refuses food at the sight of the car, go slowly and consider medication.

## Eating poop (coprophagia)

**Why it works:** nobody fully knows. It is common, usually harmless, and disgusting. Puppies often grow out of it.

1. Vet check for gut problems, parasites, and diet, especially if it is new in an adult.
2. Pick up immediately; the dog cannot eat what is not there.
3. Teach "leave it" with rewards and reward the dog for walking away from poop on walks. Keep it on a leash in the yard until the habit fades.
4. Food additives sold to stop this have weak evidence. Ask your vet before spending.

**Avoid:** punishment. It teaches the dog to eat it faster, or to eat it out of your sight.
