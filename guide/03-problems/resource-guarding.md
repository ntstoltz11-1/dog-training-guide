# Resource guarding

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus for the protocol; Moderate for the warning against force (clinic survey data).
**See a vet first?** Yes. Mouth pain and gut pain make dogs guard food.

## What it is

Your dog stiffens, eats faster, growls, snaps, or bites when someone approaches its food, a chew, a toy, a bed, or sometimes a person. Guarding is normal dog behavior. The problem is the intensity and whether it is safe for your household.

## Safety first

- **If a bite has already happened, or children live in the home, stop and get a professional** before trying anything on this page. See `guide/01-start-here/when-to-get-help.md`.
- Teach everyone in the house: nobody approaches, reaches toward, or takes anything from the dog while you are working on this.

## Before you start

- Feed the dog alone, behind a closed door or gate, in a quiet place.
- Remove items the dog guards hardest. Fewer high-value chews lying around means fewer rehearsals.
- Write down what the dog guards, from whom, and how far away a person can be before the dog reacts. That distance is your starting point.

## Do this

1. Teach a cheerful trade: hold out a better treat, say "trade", let the dog drop the item to eat, then **give the item back**. The dog learns that giving things up is not a loss. Practice with boring items first.
2. Run the approach protocol. From beyond the reaction distance, walk toward the dog while it has a low-value item, toss a fantastic treat, and walk away. Repeat. Over sessions, come closer, then use better items. Card: `guide/04-protocols/mine-protocol.md`.
3. Teach "drop" and "leave it" with rewards, separately, so you have tools for emergencies.
4. Add hand-near-bowl work only after approach is easy: drop a treat into the bowl while the dog eats, walk away. The hand becomes a delivery, not a threat.

Starting numbers: sessions of 5 minutes, several times a week. Many repetitions at each distance before moving closer. Adjust to your dog.

## Avoid this

- **Taking things away "to show who is boss"**: in clinic data, forcing an item from a dog's mouth triggered aggression in 39% of dogs, and the dog learns to guard harder.
- **Putting your hand in the bowl to "get the dog used to it"**: the dog gets used to defending the bowl.
- **Punishing the growl**: you remove the warning, not the feeling. A dog that cannot growl bites.

## How long it takes

Weeks to a few months for food and toys. Guarding of people or spaces is slower and more often needs a professional. Progress looks like a softer body, slower eating, and the dog looking up expectantly when you approach.

## Get help if

- Any bite that broke skin
- Children in the home
- The dog guards a person or a whole room
- Guarding is getting worse despite the plan
