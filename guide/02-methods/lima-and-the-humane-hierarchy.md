# LIMA and the Humane Hierarchy

**Verdict:** Recommended as the decision framework for every problem in this guide.
**Evidence:** Consensus (it is an ethics standard, not a technique) resting on the reward-based evidence base.

## What it is

LIMA stands for **Least Intrusive, Minimally Aversive**. It is the ethics rule used by the main trainer certification bodies. The Humane Hierarchy is the ladder that puts it into practice: you start at the gentlest rung and only move down if the one above has honestly been tried.

## The ladder

| Rung | What you do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Health, diet, environment | Rule out pain and illness; fix the dog's living situation | Vet visit; more sleep; less time alone |
| 2. Change what comes before | Arrange things so the behavior does not get triggered | Close the curtains; walk at quiet times; gate the kitchen |
| 3. Reward the behavior you want | Pay generously for the right thing | Treat for four paws on the floor |
| 4. Reward an alternative | Pay for a different behavior that replaces the problem | Sit instead of jump; mat instead of door-dashing |
| 5. Remove rewards, let it fade, or remove pressure when the dog complies | Turn away from jumping; stop walking when the leash tightens | |
| 6. Add something unpleasant | Last resort only, with a professional, and this guide does not reach it | |

Most problems are solved on rungs 1 to 4. If you find yourself at rung 5 or 6, go back to rung 1 and check for pain.

## What the research says

- The best-known review of training-method studies (17 studies) recommended exactly this approach.
- The rung-1 step is not symbolic: around a third of dogs referred for behavior problems have pain involved, and treating it first changes outcomes.
- The welfare studies suggest the share of aversive methods matters more than the specific tool: a little pressure mixed into reward training still raised stress compared with reward-only training.

## Strengths

- Gives you a procedure, not just a philosophy
- Forces the vet check and management steps people skip
- Shared language with certified professionals, so you can ask a trainer "which rung are we on?"

## Risks and side effects

- The "last resort" rung can become a habit if "we tried rewards" means one afternoon. Honest effort on rungs 1 to 4 means weeks, with measurement.
- Some force-free trainers reject rungs 5 and 6 entirely. This guide never recommends rung 6 and treats rung 5 as management, not punishment.

## Skill needed

Judgment more than mechanics. The hard part is admitting a plan is not working and going back up the ladder instead of down it.

## Legal notes

None.

## Best fit

Choosing a professional, writing a plan for a behavior problem, and deciding what to try next when something stalls.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping rung 1 because the dog "seems fine". Pain hides.
- Treating management (rung 2) as giving up. It is the foundation that lets training work.
- Jumping to rung 5 because rung 3 was tried with low-value rewards in a hard environment.
