# Relationship, games, and choice: Control Unleashed, BAT, and Do As I Do

**Verdict:** Useful, especially for reactive, fearful, and over-excited dogs. Built on reward-based training.
**Evidence:** Consensus for Control Unleashed and BAT (practitioner protocols, no controlled trials); Moderate for Do As I Do on specific tasks.

## What it is

A family of approaches that add structure, predictability, and dog choice on top of rewards. The common thread: the dog is kept below threshold, given something clear to do, and allowed to gather information and move away instead of being pushed through a scary situation.

## The three you will hear about

| Approach | Core idea | Signature exercises | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Control Unleashed** | Teach the dog to notice a trigger and stay in a thinking state through predictable games | Look at That (mark the dog for glancing at the trigger), mat work, pattern games ("1-2-3" walking, "Up-Down") | Reactivity, over-excitement, sport dogs in busy places |
| **BAT 2.0 (Behavior Adjustment Training)** | On a long line at a safe distance, the dog explores and chooses to disengage from the trigger; the handler follows rather than directs | Set-ups with a decoy at distance, "mark and move" when the dog needs help | Dog-dog and dog-human reactivity, fear |
| **Do As I Do** | The dog learns to imitate a human action on cue ("do it!") | Owner demonstrates, dog copies | Teaching tricks and object tasks; a bonding exercise |

Step cards: `guide/04-protocols/look-at-that.md`, `pattern-games.md`, `bat-setups.md`.

## How it works

Underneath, these use the same learning principles as the rest of the guide: pairing triggers with good things so the feeling changes, rewarding an alternative behavior, and letting distance itself be a reward for calm choices. The addition is predictability. A dog that knows what happens next has less to be anxious about.

## What the research says

- **Control Unleashed and BAT:** widely taught by certified trainers and veterinary behaviorists; many case reports; no published controlled trials located. That is a gap in the research, not evidence that they fail.
- **Do As I Do:** peer-reviewed experiments found dogs learned some object-related tasks as fast as or faster than with shaping. Moderate evidence for those tasks; thin as a general training method.

## Strengths

- Keeps the dog under threshold by design, so the welfare risk is low
- Gives owners something concrete to do during a scary moment instead of freezing or yanking
- Pattern games travel well: once learned at home they work on the street

## Risks and side effects

- Working too close to the trigger turns any of these into flooding (overwhelming the dog). Distance is the whole method.
- BAT set-ups need a helper with a calm decoy dog and space; hard to arrange alone.

## Skill needed

Moderate to high. The mechanics are easy; reading the dog's threshold in real time is the skill. `guide/01-start-here/body-language-and-threshold.md` is the prerequisite.

## Legal notes

None.

## Best fit

Reactive and fearful dogs; adolescent dogs that cannot settle in public; any dog whose owner wants a plan for walks.

## Common mistakes

- Starting at the distance where the dog already reacts
- Rewarding only when the dog looks away, which teaches the dog to stare harder first (reward the glance at the trigger too)
- Running a set-up with a decoy dog that is itself reactive
