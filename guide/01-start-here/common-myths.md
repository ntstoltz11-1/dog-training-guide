# Common myths, and what the evidence says

Short answers to the things people will tell you at the park. Each one links to the page with the full picture.

**Evidence:** varies by item; the label is given for each.

| You will hear | What is true | Evidence | Read more |
|---|---|---|---|
| "You have to be the alpha." | The alpha-wolf idea came from captive, unrelated wolves. Wild packs are families. Dogs are not plotting for status; they are doing what has paid off. | Strong | `guide/02-methods/dominance-and-compulsion.md` |
| "He knows what he did. Look at the guilty face." | In an experiment, dogs showed the "guilty look" when their owners scolded them, whether or not they had done anything. Innocent dogs that were scolded looked guiltiest. It is appeasement to an angry human, not remorse. | Moderate | `guide/01-start-here/the-basics-in-10-minutes.md` |
| "Treats are bribes. He should do it because he respects you." | A bribe is food shown before the behavior. A reward is food after. Dogs, like people, repeat what pays. Rewards fade to intermittent and to real-life paychecks over time. | Strong (learning science) | `guide/02-methods/reward-based-training.md` |
| "Positive training is fine for tricks, but you need corrections for real reliability." | In the controlled comparisons, including one on recall in dogs with chasing problems, reward-trained dogs did as well or better than shock-collar-trained dogs. No study shows corrections adding reliability. | Moderate | `guide/02-methods/balanced-training.md` |
| "It is the breed. Those dogs are just like that." | Breed explains about 9% of behavior differences between individual dogs. How easily a dog is provoked is nearly unpredictable from breed. | Strong | `guide/05-development/breed-and-genetics.md` |
| "Let them work it out." | Dogs that are allowed to fight learn that fighting works. Separate, then reintroduce slowly. | Consensus | `guide/03-problems/aggression.md` |
| "A tired dog is a good dog." | Exercise helps, but a dog run to exhaustion daily can become fitter and more wired. Sleep and mental work calm dogs more than mileage. | Consensus | `guide/03-problems/impulse-control-and-overarousal.md` |
| "Comforting a scared dog rewards the fear." | Fear is an emotion, not a chosen behavior; you cannot reinforce it with kindness. Calm comfort during a storm is fine. | Consensus (and basic learning theory) | `guide/03-problems/fear-and-noise-phobia.md` |
| "Rub his nose in it." | The dog cannot connect a puddle from an hour ago to your anger. It learns to hide when it eliminates, including from you outside. | Consensus | `guide/03-problems/house-training.md` |
| "Wait until vaccines are done to socialize." | Veterinary behaviorists say start before the series is complete, carefully. The socialization window closes around 14 weeks; behavior problems kill more young dogs than disease. | Consensus (position statement) | `guide/05-development/puppies-and-socialization.md` |
| "Three days, three weeks, three months." | A useful reminder that settling takes time. Not a studied timeline; dogs vary widely. | Thin | `guide/05-development/adopted-adult-dogs.md` |
| "A growl is disrespect. Correct it." | A growl is a warning. Punish it and you get a dog that bites without warning. Thank the growl, fix the cause. | Consensus; Moderate against confrontation | `guide/03-problems/aggression.md` |
| "Shock collars are banned in the UK now." | Banned in Wales since 2010. Not banned in England or Scotland as of 2026-10-08, despite a planned 2024 law that never took effect. | Legal, High confidence | `guide/02-methods/balanced-training.md` |
| "You need a clicker to train properly." | A clicker and a spoken "yes" work equally well in the studies. Consistency matters, the device does not. | Moderate | `guide/04-protocols/marker-training-basics.md` |
| "Crate him so he gets used to being alone." | No good evidence that crates help separation panic, and some dogs injure themselves. Test on video. | Thin | `guide/03-problems/separation-anxiety.md` |
| "Dogs only misbehave because they are spoiled or stubborn." | Around a third of dogs referred for behavior problems have pain involved. Rule it out first. | Moderate | `guide/03-problems/first-rule-see-the-vet.md` |

## One habit that replaces all of these

When your dog does something you do not like, ask two questions instead of assigning a motive: **What did the dog get out of it?** and **How do I prevent it next time while I teach something better?**
