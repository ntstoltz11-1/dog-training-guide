# Glossary

Plain definitions of every term used in this guide. Alphabetical.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Aversive** | Anything the dog works to avoid: pain, startling noise, pressure, a scary tool. |
| **Balanced training** | A style that uses rewards to teach and punishment or pressure (leash corrections, prong, shock) to enforce. |
| **BAT (Behavior Adjustment Training)** | A reactivity method where the dog explores at a safe distance on a long line and is allowed to move away from triggers. |
| **Capturing** | Rewarding a behavior the dog does on its own (marking the moment it lies down, for example) until it happens on cue. |
| **Classical conditioning** | Learning by association: one thing predicts another. The leash predicts a walk. |
| **Counterconditioning** | Changing what a scary or exciting thing predicts, usually by pairing it with great food, so the feeling changes. |
| **Cue** | The signal (word or gesture) that asks for a behavior. "Sit" is a cue. |
| **Desensitization** | Presenting a trigger at a low enough level that the dog stays calm, then increasing slowly. |
| **Differential reinforcement** | Rewarding a different behavior instead of the one you want to stop. Reward sitting instead of jumping. |
| **Extinction** | A behavior fading because it stops paying off. Expect a temporary spike first (the extinction burst). |
| **Fear Free** | A veterinary handling approach that minimizes fear, anxiety, and stress during care. |
| **Generalization** | The dog performing a behavior in new places, with new people, around new distractions. Dogs are poor at this; it must be trained. |
| **Humane Hierarchy** | A decision ladder for trainers: health and environment first, then rewards, then alternatives, and only as a last resort anything aversive. |
| **LIMA** | Least Intrusive, Minimally Aversive. An ethics standard used by major trainer certification bodies. |
| **Luring** | Guiding the dog's body with food so the behavior happens, then rewarding. Faded quickly or the dog learns to wait for the food. |
| **Management** | Arranging the environment so the behavior cannot be practiced: gates, leashes, distance, closed curtains. |
| **Marker** | A word or click that means "that exact moment earned a reward". Bridges the gap between behavior and treat. |
| **Negative punishment** | Taking away something the dog wants to reduce a behavior. You turn away when the dog jumps. |
| **Negative reinforcement** | Removing something unpleasant when the dog does the right thing. Pressure stops when the dog complies. |
| **Operant conditioning** | Learning by consequence: what happens after a behavior changes how often it happens. |
| **Positive punishment** | Adding something unpleasant to reduce a behavior. A leash jerk or shock. |
| **Positive reinforcement (R+)** | Adding something the dog wants to increase a behavior. A treat for sitting. |
| **Premack principle** | A thing the dog wants to do (sniff, run) can be the reward for a thing you asked for (come, sit). |
| **Proofing** | Practicing a behavior with increasing distance, duration, and distraction until it holds up anywhere. |
| **Reactivity** | Barking, lunging, or growling at a trigger (dogs, people, cars), usually from fear or frustration. |
| **Resource guarding** | Protecting food, toys, places, or people with stiffening, growling, or biting. |
| **Separation anxiety** | Panic when left alone or apart from a specific person. Distinct from boredom. |
| **Shaping** | Rewarding small steps toward a behavior until the full behavior appears. |
| **Socialization** | Positive exposure to people, dogs, places, sounds, and handling, most powerful between roughly 3 and 14 weeks of age. |
| **Targeting** | Teaching the dog to touch something (your hand, a mat) with its nose or paw. A versatile building block. |
| **Three Ds** | Distance, duration, distraction. Raise one at a time. |
| **Threshold** | The point past which a dog can no longer think, eat, or respond. Training happens below it. |
| **Trigger** | Whatever sets off the behavior: another dog, a doorbell, a stranger, a sound. |
| **Trigger stacking** | Stress from several events adding up until the dog overreacts to something it normally tolerates. |
| **Veterinary behaviorist** | A veterinarian with specialist training in behavior who can diagnose and prescribe. DACVB in the US. |
