# Equipment

What to buy, what to skip, and why. You need less than the pet store suggests.

**Evidence:** Consensus for most items. Legal notes are dated; see `evidence/legal-status.md` for the full table.

## Buy these

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flat collar with ID tag | Identification | Not for leash control if the dog pulls |
| Front-clip or Y-shaped harness | Spreads pressure across the chest; the front clip turns a pulling dog back toward you | Fit so two fingers slide under every strap; check for rubbing behind the front legs |
| 6 foot (about 2 m) leash | Everyday walks | Leather or biothane is easier on hands than thin nylon |
| 15 to 30 foot (5 to 10 m) long line | Recall practice and safe exploring | Use with a harness, never a collar, to protect the neck |
| Treat pouch | Fast reward delivery | Timing depends on having food in hand, not in a pocket |
| Soft, pea-sized high-value treats | Fast to eat, so you can reward often | Cheese, chicken, hot dog, commercial soft treats. Budget treats at about a tenth of daily food. |
| Basket muzzle | For any dog with a bite history, and useful for vet visits | Must allow panting and treats. Condition it slowly: `guide/04-protocols/muzzle-training.md` |
| Baby gates, exercise pen, crate | Management: stop the behavior being practiced while you train | A crate is a tool, not a cure. See `guide/03-problems/crate-training.md` |
| Food puzzles, lick mats, chews | Enrichment and calm | Cheap versions work: a towel with kibble rolled in it |
| A notebook or phone note | Tracking | `guide/06-tools/training-log.md` |

## Optional

- **Clicker.** A clear, consistent marker. Three studies found no learning advantage over a word like "yes" or over food alone, though one found clicker-trained behaviors were slower to fade. Its real advantage is consistency when several people train the dog.
- **Head halter.** Gives steering for a strong dog. Many dogs dislike it at first; condition it like a muzzle. Never jerk a head halter.
- **Visual barriers** (window film, a car crate cover) for alert barking and car reactivity.

## Skip these

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Prong (pinch) collar | Works by pain. Not shown to outperform reward-based methods in any controlled comparison, and linked to stress and aggression risk. Restricted in some countries. |
| Choke chain | Same, with added neck-injury risk; a case report describes brain injury after a "hanging" correction. |
| Shock, "e-", or "remote" collar | Not more effective than reward-based training in the controlled field studies; more stress behaviors. Banned in Wales and several European countries. **England has not banned them as of 2026-10-08**, despite common belief. |
| Citronella or shock bark collars | European veterinary behaviorists state there is no credible evidence justifying them. Barking has a cause; the collar treats the symptom. |
| Retractable leash | Teaches pulling (the leash is always tight), burns hands, and gives no control near triggers. |
| "Calming" supplements as a main plan | Evidence for most is limited. Fine to ask your vet; do not expect them to replace training. |

## Fit and safety checks

- Harness: two fingers under every strap; no rubbing in the armpit after a walk.
- Collar: two fingers under it; tag does not catch on crate bars (use a breakaway collar or remove it in the crate).
- Muzzle: dog can open its mouth fully, pant, drink, and take a treat through the front.
- Long line: only on a harness; wear gloves; never wrap it around your hand.
