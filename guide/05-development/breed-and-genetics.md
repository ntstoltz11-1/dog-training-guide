# Breed and genetics: what they do and do not predict

**Evidence for this page:** Strong. Based on a large genomic and survey study of over 18,000 dogs published in a leading journal.
**See a vet first?** Not relevant here, except that some breeds carry health conditions that affect behavior.

## The short version

Breed matters less than people think. In the largest study of its kind, breed explained about **9% of the differences in behavior between individual dogs**. Most behavior traits are inherited to some degree, but the inheritance does not line up neatly with breed. Two dogs of the same breed can be more different from each other than two dogs of different breeds.

## What breed does predict, a bit

- **Biddability** (how readily a dog takes direction from people) tracks breed ancestry more than other traits. Herding and retrieving lines tend to score higher.
- Some physical and functional traits (howling, retrieving, pointing) show breed patterns.

## What breed does not predict well

- **How easily a dog is provoked.** The study calls this "agonistic threshold" (how much it takes to trigger a growl, snap, or bite). Breed was almost uninformative for it. Breed-specific laws rest on a weak foundation.
- **Fearfulness, sociability with strangers, trainability on specific tasks.** Individual variation swamps breed averages.

## Mixed breeds

Behavior in mixed-breed dogs follows the same pattern: ancestry nudges probabilities, individual experience and temperament do the rest. A DNA test tells you ancestry, not personality.

## What this means for training

1. **Train the dog in front of you.** A "stubborn" breed label is not a reason to reach for force. A "friendly" breed label is not a reason to skip socialization.
2. **Use breed knowledge as a hint, not a verdict.** A dog from herding lines may enjoy work and need more mental exercise; a dog from guarding lines may need more careful introductions. Check the hint against the actual dog.
3. **Do not excuse behavior with breed.** "He's a terrier, he just bites" is a management failure, not biology.
4. **Expect variation within a litter.** Siblings can be bold and shy, calm and wired.

## Where genetics does matter

Inherited health conditions (joint disease, skin allergies, eye and ear problems, some neurological conditions) are more breed-linked than behavior is, and pain and sensory loss drive behavior. Know your breed's health risks and work with your vet.
