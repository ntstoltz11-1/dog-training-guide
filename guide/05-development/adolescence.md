# Adolescence (about 6 to 18 months)

**Evidence for this page:** Moderate. One well-designed study of 69 dogs tested at 5, 8, and 12 months; the sample was guide-dog candidates of a few breeds, so the picture may differ for other dogs.
**See a vet first?** Only if changes are sudden or extreme. Adolescence itself is not a medical problem.

## What is happening

Your well-trained puppy stops listening. Recall evaporates, the dog "forgets" cues, and it tests boundaries. This is not stubbornness or dominance; it is a developmental phase that looks remarkably like human adolescence. The brain is reorganizing, hormones are shifting, and the dog is more sensitive to everything.

## What the research found

- At about 8 months, dogs took longer to respond to "sit" from their **own caregiver**, but not from a stranger. Caregivers rated them as less trainable than at 5 or 12 months.
- The effect was stronger in dogs with a less secure attachment to their person.
- It was **temporary**. By 12 months, responsiveness had returned.

In plain terms: your dog is not ignoring everyone, just you, and it passes.

## Do this

1. **Lower your criteria.** Ask for things the dog can do. Reward more often than you think necessary.
2. **Go back to the long line** for recall. Do not give the dog the chance to practice ignoring you.
3. **Keep training short and fun.** Three minutes, several times a day, with games.
4. **Protect the relationship.** Play, sniffing walks, calm time together. The study points to attachment as the buffer.
5. **Manage.** Gates, leashes, and crates prevent the big mistakes (counter-surfing, bolting, fights at the park).
6. **Keep socializing**, carefully. A second sensitive period for fear can appear in adolescence; new scary things need the same gentle approach as in puppyhood.
7. **Sleep and arousal.** Adolescents often need more rest than they take. See `guide/03-problems/impulse-control-and-overarousal.md`.

## Avoid this

- **Punishment for "disobedience"**: the research suggests the dog is struggling with the relationship, not defying it. Punishment makes that worse.
- **Giving up on training**: the behaviors you reward now are the adult dog's habits.
- **Off-leash freedom as a test of trust**: it is not a test; it is a gamble.

## How long it takes

Most dogs settle noticeably between 12 and 18 months, later in large breeds. The dog that comes out the other side is the one you built during this phase.
