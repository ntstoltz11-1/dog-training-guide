# Senior dogs

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus for training and enrichment; Moderate to Thin for treatments of cognitive decline.
**See a vet first?** Yes, and regularly. Most "behavior changes" in old dogs are medical: arthritis, dental disease, failing eyes or ears, kidney and bladder changes, or cognitive decline.

## What changes

Older dogs slow down, sleep more, and can become grumpy, clingy, restless at night, or confused. Many of these are pain or sensory loss showing up as behavior. A dog that snaps when touched on the hips, or barks at a doorway it used to walk through, is telling you something.

## Cognitive decline

Dogs can develop a dementia-like condition. Vets use a checklist often summarized as **DISHA**:

| Sign | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **D**isorientation | Stuck in corners, staring at walls, lost in familiar places |
| **I**nteraction changes | Less interest in greeting, or new clinginess |
| **S**leep-wake changes | Up at night, pacing, barking at nothing |
| **H**ouse soiling | Accidents from a dog that was reliable |
| **A**ctivity and anxiety | Restlessness, new fears, or apathy |

Any of these is a vet visit. Pain and cognitive decline often overlap; a pain trial is worth asking about.

## Do this

1. **Pain first.** Ask the vet about joint pain even if the dog is not limping.
2. **Adjust the environment.** Rugs on slippery floors, ramps, night lights, raised bowls, a bed in a warm quiet spot.
3. **Keep training.** Old dogs learn new things with rewards, and gentle mental work (nose games, simple tricks) is linked to better aging. Keep sessions short and the dog comfortable.
4. **Enrichment over exercise.** Sniffing walks, food puzzles, scatter feeding. Less distance, more interest.
5. **Predictable routine.** Same times, same places. Confused dogs do better with structure.
6. **Protect sleep.** A dog awake at night may be in pain, needing the toilet, or disoriented. Each has a different fix.
7. **Ask about treatment.** Diets, supplements, and medication for cognitive decline exist; the evidence is modest, and your vet can say what is worth trying.

## Avoid this

- **Calling it "just old age"**: pain and treatable conditions hide behind that phrase.
- **Punishing accidents or night barking**: the dog cannot help it and may not understand.
- **Big changes** (moving the bed, new furniture layout) without need.
