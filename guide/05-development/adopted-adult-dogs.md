# Adopted adult dogs

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus for the approach. The popular "3-3-3 rule" has no peer-reviewed basis and is labeled Thin.
**See a vet first?** Yes. Many adopted dogs arrive with undiagnosed pain, parasites, or dental disease, and shelter records are often incomplete.

## What to expect

An adult dog arriving in a new home is stressed, however calm it looks. Some shut down and seem perfect for a week, then "change" as they relax and their real personality and habits appear. Others are frantic from day one. Both are normal.

## About the 3-3-3 rule

You will hear: 3 days to decompress, 3 weeks to learn the routine, 3 months to feel at home. It is a useful reminder that settling takes time. It is not science; no study has tested it, and dogs vary enormously. Use it as a rough mindset, not a schedule.

## Do this

**First two weeks: low demand, high predictability**
1. Same schedule every day: meals, walks, rest, bed.
2. Limit visitors and outings. The house, the yard, quiet walks.
3. Give the dog a safe space it can retreat to, and let it.
4. Hand-feed part of meals and start marker training with easy things: name, eye contact, sit. Two minutes at a time.
5. Treat house training as if the dog knows nothing, even if the shelter said it was trained. New house, new rules.
6. Leash on a harness everywhere outside, including the yard, until you know the dog will not bolt or climb.

**Weeks two to eight: build**
7. Add one new thing at a time: a new route, one calm visitor, a car ride.
8. Start formal training on the behaviors you want for life.
9. Watch for problems that only appear once the dog relaxes: guarding, reactivity, separation panic. Address them early with the relevant problem page.

## Avoid this

- **Testing the dog**: taking food away, hugging, inviting the whole family over on day two. You are collecting information the dog does not want to give yet.
- **Assuming history**: "he must have been abused" explains little and can lead to excusing behavior that needs training.
- **Off-leash too soon**: a dog with no bond and no recall is a dog that disappears.
- **Deciding the dog is "fine" in week one**: the quiet dog is often the one holding its breath.

## How long it takes

Weeks to months for the dog to show its real self and settle into routines. Some dogs take a year to fully relax. Progress looks like play, deep sleep in open rooms, and the dog seeking you out.

## Get help if

- Any aggression toward household members
- Panic when left alone
- The dog is not eating after several days
