# Puppies and socialization (about 8 weeks to 6 months)

**Evidence for this page:** Consensus (veterinary position statement) on early socialization; the sensitive period itself is well established in developmental research.
**See a vet first?** Yes, for the normal vaccination series and a baseline check. Discuss socialization timing with your vet; the position below is from a veterinary behavior body.

## The window that matters

Between roughly 3 and 14 weeks of age, puppies are built to accept new things. After that window, new experiences are met with more caution. What a puppy meets calmly in this period (people of all kinds, dogs, surfaces, sounds, handling, car rides) shapes the adult dog more than any training later.

## The vaccine question

Puppies are not fully vaccinated until about 16 weeks, so owners are often told to keep them home. The veterinary behavior position is the opposite: behavior problems are a greater threat to a young dog's life than infectious disease, and socialization should begin before the vaccine series is complete. Puppy classes can start at 7 to 8 weeks, with at least one vaccine given 7 or more days before the first class and a first deworming.

Be sensible: carry the puppy in places with unknown dogs, avoid dog parks and areas with sick dogs, and use clean floors in friends' homes and well-run puppy classes.

## What good socialization looks like

Quality beats quantity. Every new thing should end with the puppy relaxed or curious, not flooded.

| Expose to | How |
|---|---|
| People | Men, women, children, hats, beards, wheelchairs, uniforms. Treats from the puppy's person, not forced greetings. |
| Dogs | Calm, vaccinated adult dogs; a well-run puppy class. Not a free-for-all. |
| Surfaces and places | Grass, gravel, metal grates, stairs, cars, elevators, the vet lobby |
| Sounds | Traffic, vacuum, thunder recordings at low volume, fireworks recordings, paired with food |
| Handling | Paws, ears, mouth, collar grabs, being lifted, all paired with treats. Start cooperative care now ([`guide/03-problems/cooperative-care.md`](../03-problems/cooperative-care.md)). |
| Being alone | Short, calm periods in a pen or crate with a chew, building slowly. |

## Do this

1. One or two new things a day, each ending well.
2. Let the puppy choose to approach. Toss treats toward the new thing; never pull the puppy to it.
3. If the puppy is scared, add distance and pair with food. Do not push through.
4. Keep a checklist. [`guide/06-tools/training-log.md`](../06-tools/training-log.md).
5. Start manners with marker training in 2 to 3 minute sessions. Sit, come, settle. Puppies learn fast and tire faster.
6. Protect sleep: 16 to 20 hours a day. An overtired puppy bites and cannot learn.

## Avoid this

- **Waiting until vaccines are done**: the window closes.
- **Flooding**: a busy market, a dog park, a party in week one. One bad scare can last.
- **Letting every stranger grab the puppy**: the puppy learns people are overwhelming.
- **Punishment for puppy things** (mouthing, accidents, chewing): see the problem pages for each.
