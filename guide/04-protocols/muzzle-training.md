# Muzzle training

**Used for:** any dog with a bite history; dogs that scavenge dangerously; vet and groomer visits; safety during behavior work
**Evidence:** Consensus. Muzzle conditioning is standard practice; the training is plain counterconditioning.
**Time per session:** 3 to 5 minutes
**You need:** a basket muzzle that lets the dog pant, drink, and take treats; squeeze cheese or peanut butter; small treats

## The idea in one paragraph

A muzzle is a seatbelt, not a punishment. A dog that wears one happily can go to the vet, meet people during training, and be safe if something goes wrong. The dog learns, in tiny steps, that the muzzle predicts food and that putting its face in it is a trick it chooses to do.

## Setup

- Fit: the dog's mouth opens fully inside it; two fingers fit between the strap and the neck; it does not press on the eyes or nose.
- Never put it on a dog for the first time when you need it. Train weeks ahead.

## Steps

1. **Muzzle appears, food appears.** Hold the muzzle behind your back, bring it out, feed a treat, put it away. Repeat 10 times. The muzzle predicts food.
2. **Nose in.** Hold the muzzle like a bowl with a treat inside. The dog puts its nose in to eat. Repeat until the dog dives in eagerly.
3. **Nose in longer.** Smear cheese inside the front so the dog keeps its nose in for several seconds. Count up over sessions: 3 seconds, 5, 10.
4. **Strap movement.** While the dog licks, lift the strap toward the back of the head and let it drop. Repeat. Then hold it there for a second. Then fasten it loosely and unfasten it right away.
5. **Fasten and feed.** Fasten the strap, feed treats through the front, unfasten after a few seconds. Build to 30 seconds, then a minute.
6. **Move and do things.** Muzzle on, go for a short walk, play a game, feed through the front. The muzzle means fun things happen.
7. **Wear it often**, not only for bad events. If it only appears before the vet, it starts to predict the vet.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The dog pushes its nose into the muzzle when you hold it out
- Loose body and normal panting with it on
- The dog forgets it is wearing it

## When to make it harder

Add seconds, then activities, only when the dog is relaxed at the current step.

## When to make it easier

Pawing at the muzzle, backing away, refusing to put the nose in. Go back a step and use better food.

## Common mistakes

- **Forcing it on**: one bad experience and the dog fights it for months.
- **A fabric muzzle that holds the mouth shut**: the dog cannot pant; dangerous in heat and useless for training.
- **Leaving it on unsupervised** or for very long periods.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog bites when the muzzle comes out (a professional can shape this safely)
