# BAT set-ups (Behavior Adjustment Training 2.0)

**Used for:** dog-dog and dog-human reactivity, fear of strangers
**Evidence:** Thin to Consensus (a published practitioner method with case reports; no controlled trials)
**Time per session:** 15 to 30 minutes
**You need:** a 5 to 10 metre long line on a harness, a large open space, a helper with a calm, non-reactive decoy dog (or a helper person for dog-human cases)

## The idea in one paragraph

Instead of directing the dog, you let it explore at a distance where the trigger is visible but not alarming. The dog sniffs, looks, moves, and gathers information at its own pace. When it chooses to look away or move off, that choice is reinforced by the relief of distance and by the freedom to keep exploring. The handler's job is to follow, keep the line loose, and step in only when the dog needs help.

## Setup

- Decoy at a distance where your dog notices but stays loose. Often 30 metres or more at first.
- Long line held with slack, in loops, so the dog can wander without hitting the end.
- Helper knows the signals for "closer", "stay", and "retreat".

## Steps

1. Let the dog wander. Follow. Keep the line slack. Say little.
2. When the dog notices the decoy, do nothing. Let it look, sniff the air, and decide.
3. If the dog looks away, sniffs the ground, or turns back toward you on its own, let it keep exploring in the direction it chose. That freedom is the reward.
4. If the dog starts to fixate (still body, hard stare, forward lean), use **mark and move**: say "yes" or "this way", move a few steps away in a relaxed arc, and feed a treat as the dog follows. Then resume wandering.
5. Over the session, the dog's own wandering will often take it closer. Let that happen only as long as the body stays loose.
6. End with an easy retreat and a sniffing break.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The dog looks at the decoy and goes back to sniffing
- The dog chooses to approach a little and then chooses to leave
- Recovery after a startle is quick

## When to make it harder

New decoy, a moving decoy, a slightly smaller space, over several sessions.

## When to make it easier

Fixating on most looks, refusing to sniff, pulling toward or away hard. More distance, calmer decoy, shorter session.

## Common mistakes

- **Steering the dog**: BAT is about the dog's choices. A tight line turns it into a leash walk past a trigger.
- **Starting too close**: a dog that reacts in a set-up has learned nothing except that set-ups are scary.
- **A reactive decoy**: both dogs need to be below threshold.
- **Using it as the only tool**: pair with Look at That and counterconditioning for everyday walks.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog has bitten
- You cannot arrange a calm decoy and space (a trainer can)
