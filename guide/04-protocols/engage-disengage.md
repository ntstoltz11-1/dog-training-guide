# Engage-disengage

**Used for:** reactivity and fear; a close cousin of Look at That that adds a second stage
**Evidence:** Consensus (widely taught; no controlled trials)
**Time per session:** 5 to 10 minutes
**You need:** marker, high-value treats, a trigger at a controllable distance

## The idea in one paragraph

Two stages. First, like Look at That, you mark the dog for noticing the trigger. Second, once that is easy, you wait a beat and mark the dog for choosing to look away on its own. The dog learns to see something and let it go without being told.

## Setup

- Below threshold: dog can see the trigger and still eat.
- Harness, leash, loaded treat pouch.

## Steps

**Stage 1: Engage**
1. Dog looks at trigger. Mark. Feed when the dog turns to you.
2. Repeat for every look. Stay in this stage until the dog is relaxed and the look-back is quick.

**Stage 2: Disengage**
3. Dog looks at trigger. Do **not** mark yet. Count silently to 2 or 3.
4. If the dog looks away on its own (toward you, the ground, anywhere), mark and feed with a jackpot.
5. If the dog keeps staring past 3 seconds, go back to Stage 1 for that repetition: mark the look, feed, and try the pause again next time.
6. Over sessions, the dog starts looking at a trigger and immediately disengaging. That is the goal.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The dog glances and then voluntarily looks elsewhere
- Looser body, softer eyes while the trigger is in view
- The dog starts disengaging from surprise triggers on walks

## When to make it harder

Move closer or to a busier place after several sessions with reliable disengagement at the current level.

## When to make it easier

Staring longer than 3 seconds on most reps, refusing food, or stiffening. Add distance and return to Stage 1.

## Common mistakes

- **Jumping to Stage 2 too soon**: the dog needs to be relaxed in Stage 1 first.
- **Pausing too long in Stage 2**: more than 3 seconds of staring is a stare, not a look. Rescue the rep.
- **Using it over threshold**: nothing on this card works with a dog that is already barking.

## Stop and get help if

- No distance works
- The dog has bitten over this trigger
