# Relaxation Protocol

**Used for:** anxiety, over-arousal, dogs that cannot settle, a foundation before any fear work
**Evidence:** Consensus (a widely used veterinary behavior protocol; no controlled trials)
**Time per session:** 5 to 15 minutes
**You need:** a mat or bed, small treats, a quiet room, and patience over about two weeks

## The idea in one paragraph

A structured program that teaches the dog to stay relaxed on a mat while increasingly distracting things happen around it: you walk away, you clap, you jog in place, a door opens. The dog learns that staying settled while the world moves is the job, and that calm pays. It builds a "settle" you can take anywhere.

## Setup

- Mat in a quiet room. Teach "go to your mat" first with a lure and a treat on the mat.
- You reward **relaxed** positions: lying down, hip rolled to one side, head low. Not an alert sphinx pose.
- The dog is not asked to stay; it is paid for choosing to stay.

## Steps

Each day has a list of tasks you do while the dog lies on the mat. Reward after each task if the dog stayed relaxed. If the dog gets up, say nothing, guide it back, and repeat an easier task.

**Day 1 (sample)**
1. Stand in front of the dog 5 seconds. Treat.
2. Take one step back and return. Treat.
3. Stand 10 seconds. Treat.
4. Take two steps back and return. Treat.
5. Clap once softly. Treat.
6. Jog in place 3 seconds. Treat.
7. Walk a quarter circle around the dog. Treat.
8. Stand 15 seconds. Treat.

**Days 2 to 15**: the same shape with longer durations, more steps away, going out of sight for seconds, knocking on a wall, opening and closing a door, speaking in an excited voice, and finally leaving the room briefly.

The full 15-day task lists are published in veterinary behavior texts and widely available online as "Relaxation Protocol, Overall". Use any faithful version.

Starting numbers are starting points. Repeat a day until it is easy before moving on.

## How to tell it is working

- Softer body on the mat: hip rolled, chin down, slow blinks, a sigh
- The dog goes to the mat on its own when things get busy

## When to make it harder

Move to the next day's list after completing the current one with the dog relaxed throughout.

## When to make it easier

The dog gets up more than once or twice, or lies alert and stiff. Repeat the previous day, or split a task in half.

## Common mistakes

- **Rewarding alert posture**: you build a dog that watches, not one that relaxes.
- **Rushing days**: two weeks is the minimum, not the target.
- **Talking the dog through it**: the point is for the dog to settle without your help.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog cannot lie down in a quiet room at all (check for pain)
