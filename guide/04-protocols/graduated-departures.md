# Graduated departures (separation anxiety)

**Used for:** separation anxiety and isolation distress
**Evidence:** Consensus for the method (widely used, no controlled trials); medication alongside it is Moderate to Strong
**Time per session:** 20 to 30 minutes including breaks, several times a week
**You need:** a camera you can watch live on your phone, a notebook, and a way to avoid real absences while you train

## The idea in one paragraph

You leave for less time than it takes your dog to panic, come back, and repeat. Over weeks, the dog learns that departures are boring and always end. The whole method depends on never crossing the threshold during training and never leaving the dog alone for real beyond what it can handle.

## Setup

- **Suspend real absences** longer than the dog's threshold. Sitter, daycare, friend, taking the dog along. Every panic sets you back.
- Set up the camera where you can see the dog's body.
- Find the threshold on video: seconds alone before the first stress sign (pacing, whining, panting, scanning the door). Write it down.

## Steps

1. Pick up keys, put on shoes, as usual. No speeches.
2. Step out the door. Wait for **less** than threshold. If threshold is 40 seconds, start at 10.
3. Come back in. Be calm and boring. Do not greet or reward; the return is nothing special.
4. Wait a minute or two, then repeat.
5. Vary the durations within a session: 10 seconds, 15, 5, 20, 8, 25. Mostly easy, a few that stretch. If every rep is longer than the last, the dog learns to brace for it.
6. Watch the camera the whole time. If a stress sign appears, come back **before** it escalates, and make the next few reps very short.
7. End the session with a few easy reps.
8. Next session, your longest rep can be a little longer than last time, if the dog stayed calm.

Starting numbers are starting points. Progress is measured in your longest calm rep.

## How to tell it is working

- The longest calm absence grows week by week
- The dog lies down or wanders off while you are out instead of watching the door
- Pre-departure cues (keys, shoes) stop producing anxiety

## When to make it harder

Only when the dog is relaxed on video throughout a session. Then add a little time to the longest rep.

## When to make it easier

Any stress sign on video. Drop your durations by half for the next session.

## Common mistakes

- **Going to real absences too soon**: one panic can undo weeks.
- **Rewarding the return**: it makes the return the exciting event, which makes the wait harder.
- **Steadily increasing every rep**: the dog learns the pattern and braces. Mix easy and hard.
- **Training without the camera**: you cannot see the first stress sign from outside the door.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog injures itself
- You cannot arrange absence-avoidance (a professional can help build a plan)
- No increase in calm time after 6 to 8 weeks
