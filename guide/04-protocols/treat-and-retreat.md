# Treat and Retreat

**Used for:** dogs that are shy or fearful of strangers or visitors
**Evidence:** Consensus (a practitioner protocol; no controlled trials)
**Time per session:** 5 to 10 minutes per visitor
**You need:** a visitor willing to follow instructions, high-value treats, space for the dog to move away

## The idea in one paragraph

Most people try to make friends with a shy dog by offering a treat from their hand. That forces the dog to come close to get the food, which is the scary part. Treat and Retreat flips it: the visitor tosses the treat **behind** the dog, so the dog moves away to eat, then chooses whether to come back. Approach becomes voluntary, retreat is rewarded, and the stranger starts to predict good things.

## Setup

- Dog loose or on a long line in a room with space behind it to retreat.
- Visitor sits or stands sideways, no eye contact, no talking to the dog.
- You hand the visitor the treats.

## Steps

1. Visitor tosses a treat past the dog so it lands behind it. The dog turns away to eat.
2. The dog turns back and looks at the visitor. Visitor tosses another treat behind the dog.
3. Repeat. The pattern is: look at stranger, food appears behind me, I go eat in safety.
4. As the dog starts approaching more boldly, the visitor occasionally tosses a treat a little closer to themselves, then the next one far behind the dog again. Mix near and far; never a steady march closer.
5. If the dog chooses to come right up, the visitor can drop a treat on the floor by their feet and then toss one away again. No reaching, no petting yet.
6. End while the dog is still comfortable.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The dog's approaches get closer and its body gets looser
- The dog returns quickly after retreating
- Over visits, the dog greets the person at the door

## When to make it harder

New visitors, standing visitors, visitors who move, and eventually visitors who speak softly to the dog. One change at a time.

## When to make it easier

The dog retreats and does not come back, or stops eating. The visitor backs up or sits down, and you toss the treats from a distance yourself.

## Common mistakes

- **Luring the dog closer with food in the hand**: forces approach. The treat goes behind the dog.
- **The visitor talking, staring, or reaching**: every one of those is pressure.
- **Petting too soon**: the dog coming close is not consent to touch.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog has bitten a visitor
- The dog cannot eat with anyone else in the room
