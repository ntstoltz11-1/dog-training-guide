# Pattern games

**Used for:** moments you cannot control: a dog appears round a corner, a crowded sidewalk, waiting at a vet door; also over-arousal
**Evidence:** Consensus (from the Control Unleashed program; no controlled trials)
**Time per session:** 2 to 5 minutes, then used live on walks
**You need:** treats you can deliver fast, a quiet place to teach them first

## The idea in one paragraph

A pattern game is a short, predictable routine the dog knows by heart. When the world gets unpredictable, you start the pattern and the dog has something familiar to do with its brain and body. Predictability lowers stress; the dog can think, and you have a plan instead of a panic.

## Setup

- Teach each game at home for a few days until the dog clearly anticipates the next step.
- Then use it at the first sign of trouble on a walk, not after the dog has reacted.

## Steps

**1-2-3 walking**
1. Walk and count out loud: "one, two, three". On "three", feed at your side.
2. Repeat. The dog learns that "three" always means food and starts looking at you around "two".
3. Live use: a trigger appears at a distance, you start counting and keep walking. The dog has a rhythm to hold onto.

**Up-Down**
1. Drop a treat on the ground. The dog eats it and looks up at you. Mark the look, drop another.
2. Repeat. Head down, head up, head down. The dog is doing something simple and rhythmic.
3. Live use: stand still while a trigger passes and run Up-Down. Looking at the ground is also a calming signal to other dogs.

**Ping-pong (two people)**
1. Two people a few steps apart take turns calling the dog and feeding.
2. Live use: in a busy place, the dog moves between two safe people instead of fixating.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The dog anticipates the next step (looks up at "two", looks up after eating)
- You can start a game on a walk and the dog joins in instead of staring at the trigger

## When to make it harder

Play in slightly busier places once the home version is automatic.

## When to make it easier

If the dog cannot engage with the game, the trigger is too close. Add distance, then start the game.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting the game after the explosion**: it is a prevention tool. Start at the first quiet signal.
- **Never practicing at home**: a game the dog does not know is just noise.
- **Treating it as the whole plan**: pattern games manage the moment; counterconditioning changes the feeling. Use both.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog cannot eat in any public place
