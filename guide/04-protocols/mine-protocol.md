# Mine! protocol (resource guarding)

**Used for:** guarding of food, chews, toys, and stolen items
**Evidence:** Consensus (a widely used practitioner protocol; no controlled trials). The warning against force is Moderate.
**Time per session:** 5 minutes
**You need:** a range of items from boring to precious, very high-value treats, a quiet room, and no children present

## Safety first

If the dog has bitten over an item, or children live in the home, run this only with a qualified professional.

## The idea in one paragraph

The dog guards because a person approaching has meant losing the item. You rebuild the association in tiny steps: a person approaching means something **better** arrives, and the item usually stays. Start far away with items the dog barely cares about, and only move closer or use better items when the dog is relaxed.

## Setup

- Rank your dog's items from 1 (empty bowl) to 5 (the thing it guards hardest).
- Find the starting distance: how close you can be while the dog has a level-1 item and stays loose.
- Treats must beat the item the dog has. Roast chicken beats kibble.

## Steps

**Stage A: approach and toss**
1. Dog has a level-1 item. From beyond the reaction distance, walk one step toward the dog, toss a great treat near it, turn, and walk away.
2. Repeat 10 times a session. Over sessions, approach one step closer each time the dog stays relaxed.
3. Goal: you can walk right up while the dog has the item and the dog looks up happily for the treat.

**Stage B: better items**
4. Go back to the starting distance with a level-2 item. Repeat Stage A. Then level 3, and so on.

**Stage C: hand near the item**
5. With a level-1 item, approach, drop a treat right next to the item, walk away.
6. Progress to touching the bowl or item briefly while dropping the treat, then to picking it up, adding a treat, and putting it straight back.

**Stage D: trades**
7. Separately, teach "trade": show a better treat, say "trade", the dog drops the item, you give the treat and **give the item back**. Most trades return the item. The dog learns giving things up is temporary.

Starting numbers are starting points. Many sessions per stage. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The dog looks up and loosens when you approach, instead of hunching over the item
- Eating slows down
- The dog brings you items hoping for a trade

## When to make it harder

After several sessions with a relaxed dog at the current level.

## When to make it easier

Stiffening, faster eating, a hard stare, a low growl. Add distance or drop an item level immediately. A growl is information: you went too fast.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with the item the dog guards most**: start with what it barely cares about.
- **Taking the item and not giving it back**: confirms the dog's fear.
- **Punishing the growl**: removes the warning, not the guarding.

## Stop and get help if

- Any bite
- The dog guards people or spaces, not just items
- No change after 4 to 6 weeks
