# Look at That (LAT)

**Used for:** leash reactivity, dogs that stare at triggers, over-focused sport dogs
**Evidence:** Consensus (from the Control Unleashed program; no controlled trials)
**Time per session:** 5 to 10 minutes
**You need:** marker, high-value treats, a trigger at a controllable distance

## The idea in one paragraph

Instead of trying to stop your dog looking at the scary or exciting thing, you pay the dog **for** looking at it. Looking becomes a job: "see a dog, tell me about it, get paid." The dog learns to glance and check in rather than stare and explode. It combines a change of feeling (dog predicts food) with a behavior (look, then look back).

## Setup

- Start at a distance where the dog can see the trigger and still eat.
- Dog on a harness and leash, you with a loaded treat pouch.

## Steps

1. The dog looks at the trigger. **Mark immediately**, the moment the head turns toward it.
2. The dog turns back to you for the treat (the marker predicts food). Feed.
3. The dog looks again. Mark again. Feed.
4. Repeat for every glance while the trigger is in view. Rate of pay is high: a treat every second or two if needed.
5. When the dog starts glancing at the trigger and turning to you before you mark, you have the finished behavior. Mark and feed that too.
6. Over sessions, decrease the distance a few steps at a time.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- Glances get shorter and the look-back gets faster
- The dog's body stays loose while looking
- Threshold distance shrinks over weeks

## When to make it harder

After 2 or 3 sessions with quick, relaxed look-backs at the current distance, move a few steps closer or try a slightly busier place.

## When to make it easier

The dog stares and does not turn back within 2 or 3 seconds, stops eating, or stiffens. Add distance now.

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting for the dog to look away before marking**: the dog learns to stare longer first. Mark the look, not the look-away.
- **Marking late**, when the dog is already locked on. Mark the first flick of the eyes.
- **Running out of treats**: this exercise burns through food. Bring more than you think.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog cannot look away at any distance
- The trigger is a person and the dog has bitten before
