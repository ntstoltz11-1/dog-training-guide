# Marker training basics

**Used for:** everything. This is the foundation skill for every other card.
**Evidence:** Strong that timing matters; Moderate that a clicker and a spoken word work equally well (three studies agree).
**Time per session:** 2 to 5 minutes
**You need:** 20 to 30 pea-sized soft treats, a quiet room, and a marker: a clicker or a short word like "yes"

## The idea in one paragraph

A marker is a sound that means "that exact thing you just did earned a reward". It lets you be precise even when the treat takes a second to arrive. Once your dog knows the marker, you can teach almost anything by marking the moment it happens.

## Setup

- A quiet room with no other dogs or food around
- Treats in a pouch or bowl within reach, not in your hand (the hand is for marking, not bribing)

## Steps

**Charge the marker (day one)**
1. Say "yes" (or click), then immediately give a treat. The dog does not need to do anything.
2. Repeat 10 to 15 times. Pause, then do another set later in the day.
3. Test: when the dog is looking away, say the marker. If its head whips round expecting food, the marker is charged.

**Use it (from day two)**
4. Pick an easy behavior the dog already offers, such as sitting or looking at you.
5. The instant the behavior happens, mark. Then feed. Order matters: behavior, mark, treat.
6. Once the dog offers the behavior repeatedly to earn marks, add the cue word just before it happens. After a dozen pairings, say the cue, wait, mark the behavior.

**Four ways to get a behavior to mark**
- **Lure**: guide with a treat in your hand, mark when the body gets there, feed. Fade the lure within 3 to 5 reps by using an empty hand that moves the same way.
- **Capture**: wait for it to happen on its own, mark.
- **Shape**: mark small steps toward the goal (a glance at the mat, a step toward it, a paw on it).
- **Target**: teach a nose touch to your palm, then move the palm to steer the dog.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The dog's head snaps toward you at the marker
- The dog starts offering the behavior without being asked
- You are rewarding roughly every 3 to 5 seconds in a session

## When to make it harder

After about 8 of 10 correct responses at the current level, add one thing: a little more duration, a little more distance, or a small distraction. Never two at once.

## When to make it easier

Fewer than 5 of 10 correct, or the dog wanders off. Go back a step and shorten the session.

## Common mistakes

- **Marking late**: you mark the dog standing up after the sit. Mark the moment the rear touches the floor.
- **Showing the treat first**: that is a bribe. Hands empty until after the marker.
- **Repeating the cue**: say it once, wait up to 5 seconds, then help with a lure if needed.
- **Long sessions**: stop while the dog still wants more.

## Stop and get help if

- The dog refuses food in a quiet room (check for pain or stress)
