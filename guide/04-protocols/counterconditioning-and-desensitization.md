# Counterconditioning and desensitization (CC/DS)

**Used for:** fear, reactivity, guarding, handling, noise phobia, anything where the dog's feeling is the problem
**Evidence:** Consensus (standard of care in veterinary behavior); some controlled support in noise-fear studies
**Time per session:** 5 to 10 minutes
**You need:** very high-value food, control over the trigger's distance or intensity, a helper for people or dog triggers

## The idea in one paragraph

**Desensitization** means presenting the scary thing so faintly the dog barely cares, then increasing slowly. **Counterconditioning** means pairing it with something wonderful so the feeling changes from "uh-oh" to "oh good". Together they teach the dog's brain that the trigger predicts chicken. You are not training a behavior; you are changing an emotion.

## Setup

- Find the distance or intensity where the dog notices the trigger but stays loose and will eat. That is "below threshold". [Reading your dog: body language and threshold](../01-start-here/body-language-and-threshold.md).
- Arrange the trigger so you control it: a helper who walks at a set distance, a recording at a set volume, your hand moving a set amount.

## Steps

1. Trigger appears (dog notices).
2. Great food starts flowing, one piece after another, for as long as the trigger is present.
3. Trigger disappears. Food stops.
4. Repeat 5 to 10 times. End the session while the dog is still relaxed.
5. Next session, make the trigger slightly closer, louder, or longer. Only one change.

The order is the method: **trigger first, then food**. If the food comes first, the dog learns that food predicts the scary thing.

Starting numbers are starting points. Adjust to your dog.

## How to tell it is working

- The "look-back": the dog sees the trigger and turns to you expecting food
- Softer body, open mouth, tail loose while the trigger is present
- Faster recovery when something surprises the dog

## When to make it harder

After 2 or 3 sessions where the dog stays relaxed and shows the look-back every time.

## When to make it easier

The dog stops eating, stiffens, stares, or shows two or more stress signals. Add distance immediately. You went too fast; that is information, not failure.

## Common mistakes

- **Food before trigger**: reverses the lesson.
- **Food only when the dog is calm**: that makes it a behavior exercise, and the dog may never qualify. Feed whenever the trigger is present, calm or not, as long as the dog can eat.
- **Working at the distance where the dog already reacts**: that is flooding, not desensitization.
- **Rushing**: one bad session can cost a month.

## Stop and get help if

- There is no distance or intensity at which the dog can eat
- The dog has bitten over this trigger
