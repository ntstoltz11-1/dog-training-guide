# Measurement sheet: how to tell if training is working

**Evidence for this page:** the measures themselves are standard in behavior science. The 0 to 5 stress scale below is based on the Fear Free scale used in many clinics. It has been partly tested: it lines up well with another anxiety scale, but different people scoring the same dog agree only moderately, and in one 40-dog study it did not track the stress hormone in urine. Use it to compare your own dog with itself over time, not to compare dogs.

## Why measure

"Better" is a feeling. "Threshold went from 30 metres to 12 metres in six weeks" is a fact. Measurement also tells you when to make things harder (success is high) and when to back off (success is dropping).

## Pick one or two numbers per goal

| What you are working on | Measure this | How |
|---|---|---|
| A skill (sit, down, recall) | **Speed**: seconds from cue to the dog doing it | Count "one-one-thousand" |
| | **First-try rate**: how many of 10 cues worked on the first say | Tally marks |
| A stay or settle | **Duration**: seconds held | Phone timer |
| Reactivity or fear | **Threshold distance**: how far the trigger can be while the dog still eats | Pace it out |
| | **Recovery time**: seconds from a reaction back to taking food and loose body | Phone timer |
| | **Reactions per walk** | Tally marks |
| Separation | **Calm alone time**: seconds or minutes before the first stress sign on video | Camera timestamp |
| Guarding | **Approach distance** without stiffening | Pace it out |
| Anything | **Stress signals per session**: lip licks, yawns, looking away, shaking off | Tally marks from video |

## A simple stress scale (0 to 5)

Used by many veterinary clinics. Score the dog before, during, and after a session.

| Score | What you see |
|---|---|
| 0 | Relaxed, loose, takes food gently, engages |
| 1 | Mild: a lip lick or yawn, slightly closed mouth, still takes food |
| 2 | Moderate: several quiet signals, ears back, takes food hard or slowly |
| 3 | Clear stress: refuses food, panting, trying to move away, or stiff and still |
| 4 | High: trembling, hiding, frantic, or growling |
| 5 | Panic or aggression: lunging, snapping, escape attempts |

Train at 0 to 1. Stop and add distance at 2. Above 2, the session is over; make a note of what pushed it there.

## The 80 percent rule

Count 10 attempts. 8 or more right: make it a little harder. 5 to 7: stay here. Fewer than 5: make it easier. This is a widely used rule of thumb, not a tested number.

## Questionnaire option

For a before-and-after picture of your dog's overall behavior, the **C-BARQ** (Canine Behavioral Assessment and Research Questionnaire) is a free, validated owner survey from the University of Pennsylvania. Fill it in at the start and again every few months.

## Record it

Put the numbers in `training-log.md`. Re-measure every two weeks.
