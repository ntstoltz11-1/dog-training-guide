# Talking to your vet about behavior medication

**Evidence for this page:** varies by drug; see the table. This page has no doses on purpose. Your vet chooses the drug and the amount for your dog.

## When medication is worth discussing

- The dog is too scared or too wound up to eat or learn in the situations you need to train
- Panic when alone that is not shifting with training
- Noise fear that is getting worse each season
- Aggression driven by fear or anxiety, as part of a plan with a professional
- You have done 8 to 12 weeks of consistent training with little progress

Medication lowers fear and arousal enough for training to work. It does not replace training, and the best results in the studies came from the two together.

## Two kinds

| Kind | How it is used | Examples (names, not doses) | Time to work |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Every day, for months, to lower baseline anxiety | Fluoxetine, clomipramine | 4 to 6 weeks for full effect |
| **Situational** | Before a known event | Dexmedetomidine gel (Sileo), trazodone, gabapentin, imepitoin (Pexion) | About an hour (imepitoin starts 2 days before) |

## What the evidence says, in plain terms

| Drug and situation | Finding | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Fluoxetine for separation anxiety, with training | 72% of dogs improved vs 50% on placebo plus training, in a trial of 242 dogs (197 completed) | Moderate to Strong |
| Clomipramine for separation anxiety, with training | One trial of 95 dogs found the standard dose sped up improvement in destruction and soiling, not vocalizing; an earlier trial of 49 dogs found no benefit over placebo | Moderate, mixed |
| Dexmedetomidine gel for fireworks | Good or excellent effect in 72% vs 37% on placebo, 182 dogs | Strong |
| Imepitoin for fireworks | Owners about 4.7 times as likely to rate the effect good or excellent, 238 dogs; US approved 2018; side effects (wobbliness, appetite) in about half, and a few dogs became less inhibited | Strong |
| Gabapentin for storms | Small crossover trial, 18 dogs: better on average, 3 dogs worse | Thin |
| Trazodone before vet visits | 20-dog trial: some stress scores lower, stress hormone unchanged | Thin to Moderate |
| Gabapentin before vet visits | 22-dog trial: only lip licking dropped | Thin |
| Multi-drug protocols before vet visits | Lower stress scores than baseline; no placebo group | Thin |

Note the placebo groups: dogs getting training alone improved a great deal. Medication helps; training does the work.

## Questions to bring to the appointment

1. Have we ruled out pain and medical causes? ([First rule: see the vet](../03-problems/first-rule-see-the-vet.md))
2. Daily or situational, or both, for my dog's problem?
3. What should I expect to see in the first weeks, and what are the side effects to watch for?
4. How will we know it is working, and when would we stop or change?
5. Can you refer me to a veterinary behaviorist if this is complex?
6. Is there a trial period before committing long-term?

## What not to do

- Give human medication or a friend's dog's medication. Doses and safety differ.
- Stop daily medication suddenly. Ask how to taper.
- Expect a pill to fix a training problem on its own.
- Give "calming" supplements as a substitute for a vet conversation. Evidence for most is limited.
