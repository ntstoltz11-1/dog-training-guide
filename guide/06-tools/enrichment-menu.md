# Enrichment menu

Things to do with your dog's brain. Most behavior problems shrink when a dog has enough to do, enough to sniff, and enough sleep. Pick one or two a day.

**Evidence:** Consensus. Enrichment is widely recommended by veterinary behaviorists; specific claims (for example, that sniffing tires a dog more than running) are Thin.

## Food (cheapest, most effective)

| Activity | How | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Scatter feeding | Throw a meal's kibble across the lawn or a snuffle mat. The dog hunts for it. | Free |
| Stuffed food toy | Fill a rubber toy with wet food or soaked kibble; freeze it for longer work | Low |
| Towel roll | Sprinkle kibble on a towel, roll it up, let the dog unroll it | Free |
| Muffin tin puzzle | Kibble in a muffin tin, tennis balls on top | Free |
| Cardboard box | Treats inside a closed box, or boxes inside boxes. Supervise the shredding. | Free |
| Lick mat | Spread something soft on a textured mat. Licking is calming. | Low |
| Hide and seek with food | Hide a few treats around a room; "find it" | Free |

## Scent

| Activity | How |
|---|---|
| Sniffing walk | A walk where the dog chooses the route and the pace. Long line, no agenda. 20 minutes of sniffing is a real workout for the brain. |
| Scent trails | Drag a treat along the ground and leave a jackpot at the end |
| Nose work games | Hide a scented object (a tea bag in a sock) in one of three boxes; reward finding it. Grows into a sport. |

## Chewing

Dogs need to chew. Rotate three or four safe chews so something is always "new". Supervise anything that can splinter or be swallowed whole.

## Movement and play

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Tug with rules | "Take it" to start, "out" to pause (trade for a treat), sit to restart. Teaches arousal up and down. |
| Fetch in short sets | A few throws, then a sniff break. Endless fetch builds an addict, not a calm dog. |
| Flirt pole | A toy on a rope on a pole. Great for chasers; use the same rules as tug. |
| Agility at home | A broomstick on two buckets, a tunnel from a cardboard box, a mat to run to |
| Swimming | For dogs that like it; low impact for seniors |

## Thinking

| Activity | Notes |
|---|---|
| Trick training | Spin, bow, touch, paws up, chin rest. Three minutes a day. Tricks are training with no stakes. |
| Shaping games | Reward the dog for interacting with a new object in any way; let it figure out what pays |
| "Do As I Do" | Show, then "do it". A fun imitation game for dogs that enjoy it. [Relationship, games, and choice: Control Unleashed, BAT, and Do As I Do](../02-methods/relationship-games-and-choice.md) |
| New places | A hardware store that allows dogs, a quiet car park, a friend's garden. One new place a week, at the dog's pace. |

## Rest

Adult dogs often need 12 or more hours of sleep a day; puppies and seniors more. If your dog is wired in the evenings, the missing enrichment is probably sleep. A dark, quiet space after exercise is not laziness; it is part of the plan.

## Building a day

- Morning: a sniffing walk or scatter-fed breakfast
- Midday: a stuffed toy or chew during your busiest hour
- Afternoon: a three-minute training game
- Evening: a short walk, then a lick mat, then bed
