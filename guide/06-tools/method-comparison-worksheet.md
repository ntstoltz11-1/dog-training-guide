# Method comparison worksheet

For when you want to test two approaches on your own dog instead of taking anyone's word for it. This is a fair test for one dog, not a scientific study, and it will not tell you what works for dogs in general.

## Before you start

- Pick **one behavior** and define it so two people would agree whether it happened. Not "better recall" but "front feet reach me within 5 seconds of one cue, from 10 metres, with another person standing nearby."
- Pick **one main number** from [Measurement sheet: how to tell if training is working](measurement-sheet.md) (speed, first-try rate, threshold distance).
- Pick **one welfare number** too: stress signals per session, or the 0 to 5 stress score. A method that works but stresses the dog is not a win.

## Rules for a fair test

| Keep the same | Why |
|---|---|
| Who trains | Skill differences swamp method differences |
| Total minutes per method | More practice wins regardless of method |
| Reward value | Chicken beats kibble regardless of method |
| Spacing of sessions | Spaced sessions learn faster regardless of method |
| Place and distractions | Harder places lower scores regardless of method |

## Two designs that work at home

**A. Two behaviors, two methods.** Pick two behaviors of similar difficulty (say, "down" and "spin"). Teach one with Method 1 and the other with Method 2, in alternating sessions. Compare how many sessions each took to reach the goal. Weakness: the two behaviors are never exactly equal.

**B. Baseline, then switch.** Measure the behavior for 3 sessions with no change (baseline). Use Method 1 for 2 to 3 weeks and measure. Switch to Method 2 for 2 to 3 weeks and measure. Weakness: whatever comes second benefits from the practice of what came first, so run the order the other way with a second behavior if you can.

## After the test

1. Did one method reach the goal in fewer sessions?
2. Did one produce fewer stress signals or lower stress scores?
3. Four weeks later, with no practice, does the behavior still work? In a new place? (Retention and generalization matter more than speed.)
4. Write a two-line conclusion. Remember it applies to this dog, this behavior, this trainer.

## What the published comparisons did that you can copy

Measured speed and first-try rate, recorded stress behaviors from video, and had a second person score the video without knowing which method was used. If you can get a friend to score your videos blind, do it.
