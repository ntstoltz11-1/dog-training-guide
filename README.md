# The Dog Training Guide

A plain-language guide to training your dog and fixing behavior problems, built on what the research actually supports. No jargon, no coding, no prior experience needed. Start here.

## How to read a page

Every page in `guide/` follows the same shape so you can skim:

- **What it is**: one short paragraph.
- **Do this**: the steps.
- **Avoid this**: common mistakes.
- **How long it takes**: a realistic range.
- **Evidence**: how well-supported the advice is (see below).
- **Get help if**: when to stop and call a professional.

## What the evidence labels mean

Dog training is full of confident advice with little behind it. Every page here tells you how strong the support is:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Several good studies agree, or a randomized trial exists |
| **Moderate** | One controlled study, or several consistent surveys |
| **Consensus** | Veterinary behaviorists and certified trainers agree; no formal trial |
| **Thin** | Case reports, one small study, or none. Treat as a starting point, not a rule |

A small line at the bottom of each page (for example `Evidence: C-01, C-03`) points to rows in [`evidence/claims.md`](evidence/claims.md) where you can see the exact source. You can ignore it unless you want to check.

## Three things to know before anything else

1. **See a vet first for any behavior problem.** Around a third of dogs referred for behavior problems have pain driving it. Training a dog in pain does not work.
2. **Reward-based training is the default.** In every controlled comparison so far it works at least as well as methods that use pain or fear, with fewer side effects.
3. **Breed is not destiny.** Breed explains only about 9% of the behavior differences between individual dogs. Train the dog in front of you.
